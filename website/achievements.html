P04 · GHZExact under stated assumptions

Exact planar GHZ threshold for n ≥ 4

The question

How much noise can be added to the correlations of an entangled quantum state before a local classical explanation becomes possible?

Where it came from

The problem grew out of Bell inequalities: Mermin studied multipartite entanglement in 1990, Żukowski introduced tests using all measurement settings in 1993, and later studies sought better bounds using numerical optimization.

Why it matters

The threshold tells an experimenter how much visibility must remain to rule out this particular class of classical models. An exact answer avoids choosing an arbitrary finite set of measurement angles.

What was established

For four or more participants, P04 gives an exact formula for planar measurements in the full-correlation GHZ model. For four participants the critical visibility is 34/105 (about 32.38%).

κn = (π/2)n ζ(n) / [2(1 + 2−n)ζ(2n)],   n ≥ 4

Example: κ4 = 105/34,   vXY(GHZ4) = 34/105 ≈ 0.32380952.

Previous studies gave rigorous bounds for selected measurement schemes. The new formula addresses the full planar full-correlation setting, rather than only one finite choice of angles.

This concerns planar XY measurements and full GHZ correlations. It does not cover every Bell test or arbitrary measurements. Independent mathematical and historical reviews are still needed.

P04 · GHZ3Narrowed bounds; problem still open

Three-party GHZ: a narrow interval and an exact-value conjecture

The question

For three entangled particles, what is the exact smallest visibility at which all planar GHZ correlations can no longer be described by a local classical model?

Where it came from

The same Bell-test question is harder with three participants. Work published in 2023 and 2024 narrowed the answer numerically and analytically, but did not find an exact threshold.

Why it matters

A narrow rigorous interval tells us how close we are to the true answer, and provides a more demanding benchmark for future constructions of local models and Bell inequalities.

What was established

P04 improves both ends of the 2024 interval. The lower bound comes from a Bell inequality checked by 272 exact rational tests; the upper bound comes from a constructive classical model. The proposed exact formula is still a conjecture.

2.034968153548… ≤ κ3 ≤ 105ζ(3)/(2π3) ≈ 2.035329429153

0.49132095555… ≤ vXY(GHZ3) ≤ 0.49140818163…

The 2024 visibility interval was 0.49129–0.49143. P04 improves both ends. The remaining interval for κ₃ is only about 0.01775% of its upper bound.

The proposed exact value is κ₃ = 105ζ(3)/(2π³). This equality has not been proved.

The lower bound uses an analytic argument and 272 precisely specified rational checks (the K7 certificate). The verification is reproducible, but it has not received independent external proof review.

Why is the exact answer difficult? Oscillations involving the Möbius function and zeros of the zeta function obstruct a natural construction of a Bell inequality. This is not equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis.

P04 · PROBABILITYExact under stated assumptions

Optimal covariance constant under strong mixing

The question

If two groups of random quantities depend only weakly on one another, how large can the covariance of bounded complex-valued observations still be?

Where it came from

Strong mixing measures dependence between two collections of events. Dvoretzky (1972), Bradley and Bryc (1985), and subsequent work established general covariance bounds, but earlier constants were not optimal.

Why it matters

The smallest possible coefficient turns a qualitative claim of weak dependence into a precise guaranteed error bound, useful when studying dependent data and stochastic processes.

What was established

P04 proves a sharp constant of 4√2 for bounded complex observations under the stated strong-mixing normalization. For bounded real observations, the sharp constant is 4.

|Cov(X,Y)| ≤ 4√2 · α(ℱ,𝒢) · ‖X‖∞ · ‖Y‖∞

Dvoretzky's earlier factor was 2π (about 6.28). P04 obtains the smaller, sharp factor 4√2 (about 5.66). For real observations, the sharp factor is 4.

Both comparisons use the same definition of strong mixing and the same normalization. A separate literature check is still needed before claiming world priority.

P04 · 1985Partial answer; general problem open

Sharper multilinear estimates and the question from 1985

The question

With three or more dependent groups, how large can a joint multilinear interaction be compared with a measure of their dependence?

Where it came from

In 1985 Bradley and Bryc proved general inequalities but explicitly noted that their coefficients were not sharp. Finding best constants connects probability to multilinear analysis and Bell-correlation geometry.

Why it matters

Better constants reveal the actual extreme behavior of interactions and prevent bounds that overstate how much dependence may accumulate across several groups.

What was established

P04 determines the sharp real and complex factors for forms that are real-valued on real inputs; for n≥4 the complex factor has an explicit zeta-function expression. This does not settle the original 1985 problem for all complex multilinear forms.

cnreal = 2n;   cncomplex = 2nκn

The 1985 question covers all complex multilinear forms. P04 proves a sharp answer for a narrower, natural subclass. The general question, and the exact complex value at n=3, remain open.

P01 · MATHEMATICSExact under stated assumptions

Universal characteristic-function dependence profile

The question

How far can a joint probability law deviate from independence when the size of each component's mean is already fixed?

Where it came from

Independence makes the joint characteristic function equal the product of its marginal characteristic functions. Characteristic-function tests have been studied for decades; more recent work defines dependence measures directly from this difference.

Why it matters

The largest possible discrepancy tells us the natural scale of such a dependence measure. Without it, a reported value is hard to interpret or compare across dimensions.

What was established

P01 gives the exact extremal value as a formula involving only the prescribed mean moduli. The universal constants include C₃=2/√3 and C₄=9/7, with explicit equality constructions.

𝒞d(r1,…,rd) = R − cos(min{Σj arccos rj, π}),   R = ∏jrj
Cd = max0≤θ≤π/d (cosdθ − cos(dθ))

For example, C₂=1, C₃=2/√3 and C₄=9/7. There are explicit distributions attaining the bounds, and C_d increases to 2. We do not claim this resolves an independently identified old open problem.

P13 · MATHEMATICSExact under stated assumptions

Sharp quantitative stability near extremal dependence

The question

When a joint law comes close to the largest possible dependence value, must its structure also be close to a law that reaches the maximum?

Where it came from

Exact inequalities identify the ceiling, but say nothing about almost-maximizing cases. Stability theory and optimal transport supply ways to measure distance to the equality cases.

Why it matters

Real-world distributions rarely achieve a theoretical maximum exactly. Stability answers how confidently a nearly optimal numerical value reveals the underlying extremal structure.

What was established

For d≥3 and any finite p≥1, P13 bounds the Wasserstein distance to the full family of maximizers by a constant times Δ^(1/(2p)) for sufficiently small deficit Δ. The exponent cannot be improved.

distWp(P,ℰd) ≤ Kd,p Δ1/(2p)

For d≥3, finite p≥1 and sufficiently small deficit Δ, the exponent 1/(2p) is optimal. A different, more structured measure of deviation has a sharp square-root rate.

This is a sharp mathematical theorem. It has not been independently classified as a solution to a historically posed open question.
