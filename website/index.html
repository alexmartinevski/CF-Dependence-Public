THE SCIENTIFIC PROGRAMME

What CF Dependence studies

The programme originated in characteristic-function measures of dependence and now investigates mathematical interactions through their observations, constraints and extremal properties.

Research object

For a joint probability law P with marginals P₁, …, P_d, the central object is the signed interaction measure: the difference between the joint law and the product of its marginals.

μP = P − (P1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Pd)
Dd(P) = supt |φP(t) − ∏j=1d φPj(tj)|

The Fourier factorization defect is the original observable—not the boundary of the project. Other norms, harmonic representations, transport and interaction observables open different extremal questions.

Research goal

Discover new mathematical laws, prove them analytically where possible, and identify common mechanisms that explain entire classes of results rather than isolated examples.

How the project works

Explore examples, numerical experiments and counterexamples; extract invariants and essential coordinates; formulate an explicit law, prove it rigorously and test whether the mechanism transfers to other problems. Exact bounds, extremizers, equality and stability are central questions.

The publications below develop connected parts of this programme: sharp inequalities, prescribed marginals and transport, algebra, harmonic reconstruction and statistics.
